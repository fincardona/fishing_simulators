Physics model of the trolling spread simulator

This page describes the simplified physical model used by the trolling spread simulator. The goal is not to reproduce a full computational fluid dynamics simulation, but to provide a useful visual model of how fishing lines behave behind a boat while trolling.

1. Top-down two-dimensional model

The simulator uses a top-down view. The boat, rods and fishing lines move in a horizontal two-dimensional plane. This means that the model focuses on the lateral spread of the lines, line crossing, turns and the effect of sea current.

The current version does not compute the real three-dimensional diving depth of the lure. The user can enter a nominal trolling depth, but in the present model this parameter is used only as a simplified modifier of the effective inertia of the line.

2. Boat motion

The boat has a heading vector and a target trolling speed expressed in knots. Internally, speed is converted from knots to metres per second.

1\ \mathrm{knot} = 0.514444\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}

The boat does not instantly jump to the selected target speed. Instead, its water-relative velocity gradually approaches the desired velocity. This makes acceleration and deceleration smoother and gives the lines a more natural transient behavior.

\mathbf{v}_{boat,water}^{\,new} = \mathbf{v}_{boat,water} + \left( \mathbf{v}_{target} - \mathbf{v}_{boat,water} \right) k\,\Delta t

where k is a response coefficient and Δt is the simulation time step.

The boat velocity over ground is then obtained by adding the current velocity:

\mathbf{v}_{boat,ground} = \mathbf{v}_{boat,water} + \mathbf{v}_{current}

3. Sea current

Sea current is modeled as a velocity vector of the water. The user can configure its intensity in knots and its direction relative to the current boat heading.

\mathbf{v}_{current} = V_{current} \left( \cos\theta,\ \sin\theta \right)

A following current reduces the relative speed between the line and the water. A head current increases it. A side current pushes the whole line spread sideways.

4. Line as a chain of segments

Each fishing line is modeled as a chain of connected points. If the user sets a line length L and the simulation uses N segments, each segment has rest length:

l_0 = \frac{L}{N}

The first point of the line is attached to the rod tip. The other points are free to move, but distance constraints are repeatedly applied to keep neighbouring points separated by approximately the rest length l₀. This approximates an almost inextensible line.

5. Rod position and rod angle

Each rod has a lateral base position and an angle relative to the stern direction. The rod tip becomes the anchor point of the fishing line. This makes it possible to simulate side rods, central rods and asymmetric trolling spreads.

Conceptually, the rod direction is calculated from a backward vector and a right-side vector:

\mathbf{d}_{rod} = \mathbf{d}_{back} \cos\alpha + \mathbf{d}_{right} \sin\alpha

The rod tip is then:

\mathbf{x}_{tip} = \mathbf{x}_{base} + L_{rod}\,\mathbf{d}_{rod}

6. Hydrodynamic drag

The main physical force used by the model is hydrodynamic drag. For each line point, the relevant velocity is the velocity relative to the water:

\mathbf{v}_{rel} = \mathbf{v}_{point} - \mathbf{v}_{current}

The drag magnitude is modeled with the usual quadratic drag relation:

F_D = \frac{1}{2} \rho C_D A |\mathbf{v}_{rel}|^2

where:

  • ρ is the density of seawater;
  • CD is a drag coefficient;
  • A is an approximate reference area;
  • |vrel| is the relative speed between line and water.

The drag force acts in the opposite direction of the relative velocity:

\mathbf{F}_D = - F_D \frac{\mathbf{v}_{rel}}{|\mathbf{v}_{rel}|}

7. Effect of line diameter

The line diameter affects drag because it changes the approximate area exposed to the water. For a line segment, the simulator uses:

A_{line} \approx d_{line}\,l_0

A thicker line therefore creates more resistance and tends to lag more behind the boat, especially during turns.

8. Lure mass and lure drag

The lure at the end of the line has two effects in the simulation:

  • it adds extra hydrodynamic drag at the last point of the line;
  • it adds mass, making the final point more inertial.

The lure mass is converted from grams to kilograms:

m_{lure} = \frac{m_{lure,g}}{1000}

The effective mass of the last point is:

m_{eff,last} = m_{line} \left( 1 + \frac{D}{25} \right) + m_{lure}

where D is the nominal trolling depth entered by the user. In this simplified top-down model, lure weight does not determine vertical depth; it mainly affects how quickly the lure changes direction.

9. Time integration

At each frame, the simulator computes the force on each point, converts it to acceleration, updates velocity and then updates position:

\mathbf{a} = \frac{\mathbf{F}}{m}
\mathbf{v}^{new} = \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{a}\,\Delta t
\mathbf{x}^{new} = \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{v}^{new}\,\Delta t

A damping factor is applied to avoid excessive oscillations and keep the simulation visually stable.

10. Engine wake

The engine wake is a visual aid. Its length is not obtained from a full hydrodynamic model. Instead, it is interpolated from a few reference points chosen to give a plausible visual scale:

(0\ \mathrm{kn}, 0\ \mathrm{m}),\quad (3\ \mathrm{kn}, 5\ \mathrm{m}),\quad (6\ \mathrm{kn}, 15\ \mathrm{m}),\quad (12\ \mathrm{kn}, 30\ \mathrm{m})

Between these points, the wake length is computed by piecewise linear interpolation.

11. Limitations

The model is intentionally simplified. It does not include rod bending, waves, wind, propeller turbulence, three-dimensional lure diving, seabed interaction or exact lure hydrodynamics. It should be understood as a practical visual simulator, not as a certified engineering or navigation tool.
